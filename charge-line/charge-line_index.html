How to use this
The lanes
Texts that assert the charge run down the left. Rulers and councils that enforced it run down the right. The two withdrawals sit across both lanes at the bottom.
The lines
A dashed line joins a text to an enforcement that used it. Tap either card: the line darkens and a note says how.
Open a card
Tap it to read the evidence, the grade, and the source. One card opens at a time.
The photo mark
The small frame by a title means the open card shows a photograph. Tap the photograph to see it full size.
Learned
The button inside an open card marks it learned. The bar in the strip counts how many you have marked.
Filters
The dropdowns narrow the list. The i on each says what it covers.
Jump to era
Scrolls to the first card of that stretch of the run.

Filters narrows search by era, actor, grade, or photo.

Biblical passages are dated by the historical-critical consensus, the same way the Object Record dates every other document: when it was composed, what physical copy survives, and what outside witness exists for the event it describes. The crowd scene at the trial has none, so it is graded X. The text's existence by the mid-2nd century is graded A, because a fragment of John sits in a case in Manchester.

Tap any card to read the evidence, the grade, and the source. Tap a card with a line attached to bring the line forward.
